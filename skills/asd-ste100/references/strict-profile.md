# Selected strict English profile

Original operational adaptation of selected [Issue 9 passages](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf), not a replacement for the standard. Source/read scope is in [method.json](../method.json). Unread writing rules and unchecked dictionary entries remain unresolved.

## Syntax and procedure

- Prefer active voice. In descriptions, keep passive voice when the agent is unknown; inventing an agent changes meaning.
- Prefer infinitives, imperatives, simple present/past/future, and adjectival past participles. Review complex auxiliaries and ambiguous `-ing` constructions; retain necessary technical nouns and their modifiers.
- Keep articles and explicit relations that make a sentence complete. Expand prose contractions when appropriate; keep literal quotations and code intact.
- Give genuine instructions directly. Put a prerequisite before its action, normally separated with a comma. Preserve permission and possibility: converting “may open” to an unconditional “open” is a semantic failure.
- Separate successive actions; keep simultaneous actions and immediate action/result relationships together when separating them would change execution.
- Notes supply supplementary information. Keep operational limits, required actions, and safety conditions in the relevant step or safety instruction, not only in a note.

## Manual length review

Use 20 English words as the procedural-sentence ceiling; use 25 for descriptions and notes. A descriptive paragraph has one topic and at most six sentences. These are source-based editorial targets, not validated comprehension thresholds.

Review visible prose manually using the source counting conventions: numbers with units, abbreviations, identifiers, quoted text/formulas, immutable titles/labels, proper names, and hyphenated groups each count as one unit. Parenthetical material contributes one unit to the outer sentence and is checked separately. A vertical-list colon ends the introductory count; each following item is a separate count unit. Ignore document/step numbers; punctuation adds no word. Do not add hyphens or omit necessary words merely to meet a count.

Keep code outside prose counting and quotations/citations unchanged. Mixed-language counts are unresolved unless a suitable policy is available. No Korean conversion is defined. Split an overlong sentence only while preserving scope, conditions, quantities, and referents; otherwise retain meaning and record the exception.

## Vocabulary

Check the actual approved part of speech, sense, and form against an available official dictionary; use authorized domain terms under the standard's technical-term provisions. A project glossary is useful evidence but does not establish official dictionary conformity. Without the needed source, label the check unverified rather than inventing an approved term.
